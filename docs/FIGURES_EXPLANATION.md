In short, reducing user friction at the curb is twice as impactful as increasing vehicle speed."*

> **Answer**: *"4.5 km/h is the Indian IRC/MoHUA standard for urban walking speeds in mixed pedestrian environments. > **Answer**: *"Because unlike e-bikes which we filtered against major expressways and high-speed multi-lane roads (removing 437 dangerous edges), auto-rickshaws operate in mixed vehicular traffic without road class exclusions. Therefore, their accessibility reflects theoretical shortest drive times without congestion delays, representing the maximum physical upper bound."*

### Q3: *"How did you prevent population double-counting between walk and e-bike?"*
> **Answer**: *"Every H3 Res-9 cell is assigned a single deterministic population from WorldPop 2020. In our code and tables, E-Bike Uplift is defined strictly as `corr_mask & (~walk_covered) & (ebike_covered)`. A resident who is walk-covered is never counted in e-bike uplift. The union is mathematically conserved: `Walk + E-Bike Uplift + E-Auto Extra + Unserved = Total Corridor Population` (453,687)."*

### Q4: *"Why did you include Singayyanapalya and KR Puram if this is a 10-station pilot?"*
> **Answer**: *"To prevent boundary truncation bias. In spatial network analysis, if you cut off the network at the pilot boundary, residents who live 300 metres from Singayyanapalya would be falsely forced onto Garudacharpalya 1.5 km away. By including the two western stations as boundary context absorbers, 108,348 residents are correctly routed to their true station, ensuring our 10-station metrics reflect true local catchment demand."*
